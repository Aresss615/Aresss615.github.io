01 Case study · Client · Deployed

EGMC Motorparts POS

Point-of-sale and inventory for a motorcycle parts store with a repair shop. Cashiers find any part by name, brand, part number or the bike it fits, and the owner checks sales, stock and profit from the US.

My role
Solo developer: scope, build, test, deploy
Timeline
2026
Stack
Django · MySQL · HTMX · Tailwind
Status
Deployed in-store · 229 tests

01 / The problem

A parts counter lives or dies on lookup.

Customers ask for a part in four different ways: by name, by brand, by part number, or just by the bike they ride. A cashier who has to guess which way to search keeps the line waiting.

The owner also lives in the US. The shop needed a way for him to see sales, stock and profit without being there, so the system had to serve the counter and a remote owner from the same data.

Before this, the catalog lived in a paper ledger.

02 / Architecture

One app for the counter and the owner.

A single Django app serves both the cashier and the owner. Pages render on the server, and HTMX handles the interactive parts of the counter screen, so there is no single-page-app build to maintain.

MySQL holds the parts, their bike fitment, stock and every sale. The app is hosted online, which is what lets the owner open it from the US.

  1. Clients Browser
    • Cashier at the counter
    • Owner, remotely from the US
  2. Server Django app
    • Server-rendered pages
    • HTMX for the counter screen
    • Sales, stock and profit reports
  3. Data MySQL
    • Parts and bike fitment
    • Stock
    • Every sale

03 / Hard problems

What took the most thought.

  1. 01

    Search the way customers ask

    One search box covers name, brand, part number and bike fitment, so the cashier never has to choose a search mode. A customer who only knows their bike still gets an answer.

  2. 02

    An owner in another country

    Sales, stock and profit reports live in the same app as the counter, so the numbers the owner sees from the US are the sales the cashier just rang up.

  3. 03

    Tested like it handles real money

    The system runs a live store, where a wrong total or a stock count that drifts costs real money. The build carries 229 automated tests to keep changes from breaking what already works.

04 / Result

Running at the counter.

The system is deployed and running at the store’s counter, and it gives the owner his view of the shop from the US.
